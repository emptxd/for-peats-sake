Cattail Cultivation at Teichweide (Neukalen, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern)

In 2019, a 10-hectare plot of formerly drained peat meadow alongside the Teterower Peene was converted into a demonstration site for Typha (cattail) farming. With support from the landowner and under the Paludi-PRIMA project, farmers navigated permitting, site preparation and planting, and by December 2021 they harvested their first batch of chopped biomass. This pilot proved that, on just a few meters of peat depth, cattails can thrive and deliver usable biomass—all while keeping the soil wet and protecting the peat layer.

Sphagnum Farming at Hankhauser Moor (Ammerland, Lower Saxony)

Inspired by a Canadian “moss-layer transfer” technique, the first German pilot for Sphagnum (peat moss) cultivation launched in 2004 on a 1 200 m² cut-over bog. After a decade of research, the site grew to 17 ha by 2020—showing that moss can be propagated on rewetted farmland to produce peat-free substrates, restore water balance and create new income streams from specialty peat-moss products.

Large-Scale Rewetting & Grazing in the Gundelfinger Moos (Bavaria)

The ARGE Donaumoos partnership set out to rewet some 600 ha of historic floodplain grassland, restoring a near-surface water table and then introducing extensive grazing by hardy breeds (e.g. Highland cattle, water buffalo). Through a framework agreement with over 300 landowners, they secured voluntary compensation and built trust—proving that, even across large areas, rewetting plus grazing can cut greenhouse-gas emissions, enhance biodiversity and keep family farms thriving.
